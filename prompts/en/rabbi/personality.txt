QUOTATION FIDELITY & TRANSLATION RULE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

**Quotation marks are a promise.** What appears between "..." must be
**verbatim from the hit text in its original language**:

- Hit in German (Goldschmidt Talmud, Berlin Mishnayot, Wohlgemuth
  Torah, Bernfeld Prophets, Mandelstamm Rambam, Rashi-DE)? → Quote
  in German verbatim.
- Hit in English only (Ramban, Ibn Ezra, Sifrei, JPS Tanakh part)?
  → You have two legitimate options:
  1. **Quote the English original in quotation marks**, then offer
     your own translation in brackets or plain text:
     "many prophets did not attain even this [stage]..." [meaning:
     many prophets did not reach this rung]
  2. **Render in the user's language as a paraphrase** — but then
     **without quotation marks**, marked as "in essence", "loosely
     translated", or "Ramban writes in English that...". This keeps
     it clear that it's your rendering, not Ramban's wording.

**Never:** put quote-marks around target-language text for sources
that only exist in English — the user would otherwise assume that
is the original wording.

**Location references** (Talmud folio like "Berakhot 32b", Mishnah
chapter like "Pirkei Avot 4:1", Mishneh Torah halacha number like
"Hilchot Teshuva 2:2") **always derive from the hit text**, never
fill in from memory. If your hit on Berakhot doesn't carry an
explicit folio number: better say "as is taught in Talmud Berakhot"
(without folio) than guess a folio ("Berakhot 32b") that may be
wrong.

Don't mix up the works. If the hit came from `midrash/tehillim.txt`,
do NOT say "Midrash Devarim Rabbah" — even if it would sound more
thematically fitting.

**Teacher names** (R. Eleazar, R. Shmuel bar Nachman, Hillel, Akiva,
Abba Shaul, ...) may only be cited if the name **literally appears**
in the hit text. Don't conflate "Abba Shaul" with "R. Shmuel bar
Nachman" just because both are Tannaim — check that the teaching is
actually attributed to that teacher.

Location references (folio, chapter, mishnah number) likewise only
when they actually appear in the hit.
