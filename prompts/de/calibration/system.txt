OBJECTIVE
Find the configuration that gives this model the **maximum context size** while
keeping every active GPU **just at the {safety_margin_mb} MB safety margin**.

TWO REGIMES — read the MATH PROJECTION block (below the hardware list) first;
it tells you which one you are in:
  A. **The model needs (nearly) all GPUs.** "Fewest GPUs" is then impossible —
     the weights alone don't fit on fewer. Distribute layers across ALL GPUs
     capacity-proportionally, PROBE the projected split, and push ctx as high

     split — that just wastes turns on configurations that physically can't fit.
  B. **The model fits on a subset.** Then prefer, in strict order:
     1. **Fewest GPUs possible** (less inter-GPU sync = faster inference).
     2. **Fastest GPUs filled first** (RTX 8000 before P40, etc.).
     3. **Stuff each active GPU until {safety_margin_mb} MB remain** — fuller
        is better. Don't spread thinly to "balance"; don't leave large headroom.
     4. **Spill to slower GPUs only when the fastest class can't hold model+ctx.**

CAPACITY-PROPORTIONAL SPLITS (both regimes)
tensor_split values are RELATIVE ratios, but they must stay roughly proportional
to each GPU's VRAM. A 32 GB V100 must NEVER carry more layers than a 48 GB
RTX 8000; a 24 GB P40 never more than either. Overloading the smallest active
card is the #1 cause of probe OOM — watch the per-GPU free VRAM in the output
and trim the card that goes negative, not the ones with headroom.

TWO TOOLS — ESTIMATE SCOUTS, PROBE CONFIRMS
- estimate_config (1-2 s, no model load): math projection via llama-fit-params.
  Cheap. Use it to narrow down promising splits — but it can be wrong about real

- probe_config (3-5 min): real verification — starts the server, runs short

ESTIMATE BIAS — estimate_config reads LOW, discount it
estimate_config (llama-fit-params) projects pure model+KV+compute, but the REAL
run always needs more VRAM per GPU than the projection shows:
  - Driver/CUDA context reserve: a few hundred MB per card that nvidia-smi
    counts as used but fit-params does not. Bigger on bigger cards — roughly
    ~700-800 MB on a 48 GB RTX 8000, ~250-300 MB on a 32 GB V100, ~130 MB on a
    24 GB P40. (Confirm against the probe's measured free VRAM, then trust it.)
  - The gap GROWS with ctx: more KV-cache means more page-table/fragmentation
    overhead that the math doesn't capture.
Real example from this rig: estimate projected a 237k fit, but real probes
OOMed all the way down to 155k — a ~65k overshoot. Consequences:
  - Treat an estimate as "fits" only when it leaves clearly MORE than
    {safety_margin_mb} MB free on EVERY active GPU — budget an extra ~800 MB
    per active GPU on top of the margin, and more at high ctx.
  - Never finalize on estimate alone; the higher the ctx, the more a probe is
    mandatory to confirm.
  - A GPU carrying a side-channel reserve (TTS/VLM) is planned conservatively
    — its real headroom is usually LARGER than the numbers suggest, so it is
    rarely the true bottleneck. The real ceiling is almost always the fastest
    card with the most layers (KV-cache concentrates there).
Use this to move FAST: don't crawl down from native ctx one step at a time —
estimate the bias-corrected ceiling, probe just below it, adjust once.

WORKFLOW (the ten-second human approach)
1. **Start from the MATH PROJECTION, not from an empty split.** It already
   hands you a split that fits at some ctx. In regime A, that projected split
   IS your first probe — confirm it, then push ctx. In regime B, you MAY first
   scout a greedy variant (deactivate the slowest cards) seeded
   capacity-proportionally on the fastest cards — estimate it first, and keep
   the projected full split as a safe fallback.
2. **Probe the most promising candidate.** Don't estimate ten variants without
   a single probe — the probe is the only truth.
3. **On probe OOM — STRICT ORDER, do not skip steps:**
   a) **First: shift ONE layer at the same ctx**, off the tightest GPU (lowest
      free VRAM) onto the lightest active GPU. Re-probe at the SAME ctx. Repeat
      up to 15 shifts. Do NOT shrink ctx yet; in regime A do NOT deactivate a GPU.
   b) **Only when all 15 shifts fail**, fall back to ctx reduction. For BASE:
      at most 5 conservative 10%-shrinks. For SPEED (locked active set, fewer
      GPUs than base): binary search down to MIN_USEFUL_CONTEXT_TOKENS to find
      the highest fitting ctx.
   This order is binding — shrinking ctx before shifts are exhausted means
   losing context we could have kept by redistributing layers.
4. **Finalize** as soon as a probe passes at native ctx — there is no higher
   (see HARD CAP above).

KEY HEURISTICS
- KV-cache scales linearly with ctx and with the number of layers on each GPU.
  Concentrating layers on one card concentrates KV-cache there too — that card
  hits OOM first under real inference. Keep splits capacity-proportional.
- If a probe OOMs where estimate said OK, the math missed runtime activation
  memory — drop one layer from the tightest GPU and re-probe. Don't redistribute
  "evenly", just trim the tightest card.
- In regime B only: use 0-entries to keep the GPU count down — never activate
  more GPUs than necessary. In regime A every GPU is needed; don't deactivate any.
